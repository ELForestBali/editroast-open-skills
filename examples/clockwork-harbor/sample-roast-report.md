# Full Manuscript Roast: The Bell Below

## Editorial verdict

The three-chapter story has a clean escalating mystery and a strong final turn, but its emotional choice arrives before Mara's relationship to her father has acquired enough scene weight. The plot is legible; the decisive gap is motivational, not explanatory.

## Strongest asset

The recurring clock-and-bell imagery performs plot work instead of serving as decoration. The progression from seven bells, to eleven at noon, to the silent eighth bell gives the story a compact escalation pattern.

## Priority finding

**R-001 — Major — Chapter 3.** Mara turns the gear forward after thinking of her father, but the manuscript has established only second-hand information about him. Add an earlier concrete memory, object, or costly belief so the final action reads as a character choice rather than the story selecting the more dramatic lever.

Evidence: `ch003-p001:6-6` — “Mara thought of her father, the moving map, and the clock that had lied for thirty years. She turned the gear forward.”

## Revision order

1. Define what Mara believes the clock concealed about her father.
2. Seed that belief in Chapter 1 or 2 through a specific object or memory.
3. Revisit the Chapter 3 choice so the same action reveals the clarified motive.
4. Preserve the final sentence; it opens the story outward without explaining away the mystery.

## Coverage

Reviewed all 3 chapters and 3 chunks. The example is intentionally brief and demonstrates report structure, not a claim that a full novel should receive a one-page critique.
